Surgery or Endovascular Treatment in Patients with Anterior Communicating Artery
Aneurysm: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Shahab Aldin Sattari1, Ataollah Shahbandi2, Ryan P. Lee1, James Feghali1, Jordina Rincon-Torroella1, Wuyang Yang1,

INTRODUCTION
The
anterior
communicating
artery
(ACoA) is the most common site for both
ruptured and unruptured aneurysms, and
ACoA aneurysms account for 30%e37%
of all intracranial aneurysms.1-3 Existing
treatment
strategies
for
aneurysms
include
surgical
and
endovascular
management.3
Surgical approaches were the preferred
treatment option for aneurysms until the
1990s,4 when the Guglielmi detachable
coil
were
approved
for
treatment
of
aneurysms.
Since
then,
endovascular
management of aneurysms has gained
attention, especially after results of the
International
Subarachnoid
Aneurysm
Trial
showing
higher
independent
survival when endovascular approaches
are employed compared to microsurgical
clipping.4-8
In
contrast,
the
Barrow
Ruptured Aneurysm Trial showed no
signiﬁcant
difference
in
postoperative
outcomes
between
endovascular
and
surgical approaches for aneurysms.9-12
As
such,
the
optimal
treatment
for
aneurysms is still unclear,3,12-16 although
recent evidence suggests that the risk-
beneﬁt ratio of both modalities may vary
considerably depending upon the precise
location of the aneurysm.7-12,17-20
The literature comparing surgical and
endovascular
management
of
ACoA
aneurysm
in
large
patient
cohorts
is
sparse.21-24 This systematic review and a
meta-analysis sought to compare post-
operative outcomes in patients with ACoA
- BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVE: Although randomized controlled trials have
compared surgery versus endovascular treatment for intracranial aneurysms, the
literature is sparse in terms of subgroup analysis for anterior communicating
artery (ACoA) aneurysm management. This systematic review and meta-analysis
sought to compare surgical versus endovascular treatment for ACoA aneurysms.
- METHODS: Medline, PubMed, and Embase were searched from inception to
December 12, 2022. Primary outcomes were post-treatment modified Rankin
Scale (mRS) >2 and mortality. Secondary outcomes were aneurysm obliteration,
retreatment and recurrence, rebleeding, technical failure, vessel rupture,
aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage-related hydrocephalus, symptomatic
vasospasm, and stroke.
- RESULTS: Eighteen studies yielded 2368 patients, from which 1196 (50.5%) and
1172 (49.4%) patients underwent surgery and endovascular treatment, respec-
tively. The odds ratio (OR) of mortality was similar in total (OR [ 0.92 [0.63e
1.37], P [ 0.69), ruptured (OR [ 0.92 [0.62e1.36], P [ 0.66), and unruptured
cohorts (OR [ 1.58 [0.06e39.60], P [ 0.78). The OR of mRS > 2 was similar in
total (OR [ 0.75 [0.50e1.13], P [ 0.17), ruptured (OR [ 0.77 [0.49e1.20], P [
0.25), and unruptured cohorts (OR [ 0.64 [0.21e1.96], P [ 0.44). The OR of
obliteration was higher with surgery in the total (OR [ 2.52 [1.49e4.27], P [
0.0008) and ruptured cohorts (OR [ 2.61 [1.33e5.10], P [ 0.005) and unruptured
group (OR [ 3.46 [1.30e9.20], P [ 0.01). The OR of retreatment was lower with
surgery in the total (OR [ 0.37 [0.17e0.76], P [ 0.007) and ruptured cohorts
(OR [ 0.31 [0.11e0.89], P [ 0.03), thought it was similar in the unruptured group
(OR [ 0.51 [0.08e3.03], P [ 0.46). The OR of recurrence was lower with surgery
in the total (OR [ 0.22 [0.10, 0.47], P [ 0.0001), ruptured (OR [ 0.16 [0.03, 0.90],
P [ 0.04), and mixed (un) ruptured cohorts (OR [ 0.22 [0.09e0.53], P [ 0.0009).
The OR of rebleeding in ruptured group was similar (OR [ 0.66 [0.29e1.52], P [
0.33). The ORs of other outcomes were similar.
- CONCLUSIONS: ACoA aneurysms may be safely treated with either surgery or
endovascular treatment, although microsurgical clipping demonstrates higher
obliteration rates and lower rates of retreatment and recurrence.

Two authors (S.A.S. and A.S.) screened

Statistical Analysis
Baseline characteristics were presented
using descriptive statistics. The pooled
odds ratio (OR) of categorical data with a
corresponding 95% conﬁdence interval
(CI) was calculated through the Mantel-
Haenszel method using the Review Man-
ager, version 5.4.1. The heterogeneities
between studies were detected using the
I2 test statistic, which attributes the pro-
portion of total variation to the differ-
ences
between
studies
rather
than
sampling error.25 In cases of substantial
heterogeneity (i.e., I2 > 50% and P <
0.10),
the
random-effect
model
was
used; otherwise, the ﬁxed-effect model
was applied. Given the included studies
represented ruptured, unruptured, and
the mixed cohort of aneurysms, in the
case
of
unruptured
aneurysms
per
outcome,
subgroup
analysis
was
per-
formed in which the exclusively ruptured
cohorts were analyzed separately from the
mixed cohorts. All statistical tests were
conducted with a two-tailed statistical
signiﬁcance set at a P < 0.05.
RESULTS
Study Selection
The applied search strategy resulted in 704
records, from which 23 non-English re-
cords and 100 duplicate records were
removed. Five hundred eighty-one records
were screened by title and abstract and
identiﬁed 89 relevant studies. Of these, 71
articles were excluded as they were non-
comparative studies (n ¼ 31), case reports
(n ¼ 20), letters and commentaries (n ¼
6), studies without outcomes of interest
(n ¼ 9), or review studies (n ¼ 5).
Therefore,
18
studies
were
ultimately
included in this systematic review and
meta-analysis (Figure 1).30-47
Study Characteristics
The 18 studies were published between
2003 and 2022 and were conducted in the
United States (n ¼ 3), Canada (n ¼ 1),
United Kingdom (n ¼ 1), France (n ¼ 2),
Czech Republic (n ¼ 1), Hungary (n ¼ 1),
Finland (n ¼ 1), Egypt (n ¼ 1), Turkey
(n ¼ 1), India (n ¼ 1), China (n ¼ 2), and
Korea (n ¼ 3). In total, the studies yielded
2368 patients, of which 1196 (50.5%) and
1172 (49.4%) patients underwent surgery
and endovascular treatment, respectively.
Across the 18 studies, the mean age of
patients ranged from 45.4 to 64.8 years,
and 1198 (50.5%) patients were male.
Additionally, 557 (23.5%) patients pre-
sented with unruptured aneurysms, and
the median (range) follow-up duration was
9 (3e96) months (Table 1).
Risk of Bias Assessment
In terms of baseline confounders, Stekla-
cova et al.,36 Schmalz et al.,38 Backer
et al.,40 and Moon et al.42 were found to
be at high risk of bias; the remaining
studies were at moderate risk. In terms
of the remaining domains of the Risk of
Bias In Non-randomized Studies of In-
terventions, the included studies were at
moderate risk of bias (Table 2).

Summary of Key Findings
The results of this study show that surgical
and endovascular management of ACoA
aneurysm exhibit, in general, similar risk
for postoperative morbidity and mortality.
However, patients who underwent surgery
for their ACoA aneurysm had higher odds
of aneurysm obliteration and, conversely,
lower odds of re-treatment and aneurysm
recurrence (Table 3).
Morbidity and Mortality
The treatment goal for aneurysms is to
remove the aneurysm from circulation
without decreasing the baseline func-
tional status of the patient. Although
both
surgical
and
endovascular
ap-
proaches to aneurysms present risk to the
patient,3 the results of this study indicate
that the odds of post-treatment mortality
and poor mRS (i.e., mRS > 2) are similar
between
the
2
treatment
modalities.
These ﬁndings align with the results of
the Barrow Ruptured Aneurysm Trial
trial,
which
found
similar
risks
of
morbidity and mortality between surgical
and endovascular management of aneu-

line with studies by Koivisto et al. and Li
et

which
too
found
comparable
morbidity and survival albeit at a much
shorter follow-up interval at 1 year.49,50

better
outcomes
following
endovascular
management of aneurysms, a statistically

only identiﬁed along the internal carotid
territory,7,8,17 suggesting the importance
of
stratifying
outcome
studies
for
aneurysms by the location of the aneurysm.

Obliteration, Re-Treatment, and
Recurrence
Both endovascular and surgical manage-
ment for aneurysms remove the aneurysm
sac from circulation, thereby preventing
rupture and/or rebleeding.3,30 The results
of this meta-analysis show that the odds
of aneurysm obliteration was signiﬁcantly
higher for patients who underwent sur-
gery.
Furthermore,
the
odds
of
re-
treatment and aneurysm recurrence was
lower for the surgical cohort. An endo-
vascular
approach
to
aneurysms
may
elevate the risk of residual aneurysms,
which may lead to aneurysm recanaliza-
tion, growth, and recurrent SAH. As the
average rebleeding time for recurrent an-
eurysms is 8 years,10 patients undergoing
endovascular management for aneurysms
should therefore be vigilantly monitored
for re-treatment. Considering that endo-
vascular management of aneurysms is
associated with a higher risk of residual
aneurysms, young patients may be better
managed microsurgically.
Limitations
We acknowledge several limitations in this
study. Only articles with English text were
selected
from
Medline,
PubMed,
and
Embase.
Furthermore,
the
included
studies were nonrandomized and thereby
present risk of selection bias. In addition,
the total patient cohort of this systematic
review included 23.5% unruptured ACoA
aneurysm; however, a subgroup analysis
was conducted on exclusively ruptured
ACoA aneurysm to address this issue and
graded the level of the evidence of meta-
analysis outcomes for both the exclu-
sively ruptured ACoA aneurysm and over-
all cohorts. Furthermore, when it was
possible,
the
subgroup
analyses
per
unruptured aneurysm have been conduct-
ed. Additionally, the included studies had
different follow-up timelines; however, the
outcomes incorporated into the meta-
analysis was reported in range of 3e
12 months. Furthermore, evaluating the
long-term outcomes was not feasible.
Moreover,
comparing
surgery
versus
endovascular treatment solely based on
mRS as an index of the functional status is
not enough. Other factors such as quality
of life and the proportion of patients who
return to their baseline functional status
should be evaluated.
CONCLUSIONS
ACoA aneurysm can be safely treated both
via surgical and endovascular manage-
ment. Microsurgical clipping, however,
may lead to higher rates of aneurysm
obliteration and lower risk of recurrence
and re-treatment. An intention-to-treat
randomized controlled trial comparing
surgery and endovascular treatment for
ACoA
aneurysm
may
assess
which
approach is most optimal for long-term
outcomes.
